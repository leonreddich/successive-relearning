# Evidence base

Effect sizes are Hedges' g unless noted. Use these when the learner asks why the skill works this way. Do not overstate: effects vary with material and setting.

| Strategy | Key finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Practice testing / retrieval practice | Rated "high utility": benefits learners of different ages and abilities across many criterion tasks. Rereading and highlighting do not consistently boost performance. | Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 4–58 |
| Retrieval practice (meta-analysis) | 217 studies; hybrid tests (multiple choice + cued recall) most effective; matching practice and final test format helps slightly. | Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017), *Review of Educational Research*, 87(3), 659–701 |
| Retrieval practice in classrooms | 49 effect sizes, n = 5374; 57 % showed medium or large benefits across levels, subjects and formats. | Agarwal et al. (2021), *Educational Psychology Review* |
| Spaced vs. massed retrieval | g = 0.74 for spaced over massed retrieval practice (29 studies). | Latimier, Peyre & Ramus (2021), *Educational Psychology Review* |
| Optimal spacing gap | Optimal gap rises with the retention interval; about 20–40 % of a 1-week interval, falling to ~5 % for one year. | Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler (2008), *Psychological Science*, 19(11), 1095–1102 |
| Successive relearning | Retrieval to criterion across spaced sessions beats spacing alone and students' own methods on course exams and long-term retention. | Rawson, Dunlosky & Sciartelli (2013), *Educational Psychology Review*; Dunlosky et al., *Successive Relearning: An Introduction and Guide for Educators* |
| Interleaving | g = 0.42 overall (59 studies), strong for visual categories, small for math, caution with expository texts and word lists. Interleave *problems*, not text chapters. | Brunmair & Richter (2019), *Psychological Bulletin*, 145(11), 1029–1052 |
| Self-explanation | g = 0.55 (64 reports, ~6000 participants); works because learners generate inferences about causal connections. | Bisra, Liu, Nesbit, Salimi & Winne (2018), *Educational Psychology Review*, 30, 703–725 |
| Six strategies overview | Spaced practice, interleaving, retrieval practice, elaboration, concrete examples, dual coding. | Weinstein, Madan & Sumeracki (2018), *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications*, 3, 2 |
| Sleep | Total sleep deprivation after learning harms memory (g = 0.28); before learning g = 0.62. | Newbury, Crowley, Rastle & Tamminen (2021), *Psychological Bulletin*, 147(11) |

## Practical consequences

- Cramming the night before produces short-lived recall; if time is short, still split the material into at least two sessions with sleep in between.
- Successful retrieval matters: questions should be answerable after study. If a learner misses most items, restudy briefly, then retrieve again.
- Students rate interleaving and retrieval as harder and believe they learn less; tell the learner that the difficulty is expected.
